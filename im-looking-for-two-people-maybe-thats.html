The two roles

One keeps the machine running. One builds the network that feeds it.

These are counterparts, not duplicates. Switch between them to see what each one owns, what it is not, and what success looks like. Both are support-raised ministry roles rather than salaried positions.

Both are strategic, and both are opportunistic in the best sense of the word: the infrastructure for sustained, trusted engagement with frontier peoples is being built in real time, and these roles are part of that build.

The connective tissue

Neither half works alone

Joshua Project exists to help the global Church find the gaps and close them. Slide toward either extreme and watch what the work degrades into. The two open roles exist to hold the middle.

The field-facing role

A network across five world regions

The Director of Field Research and Partnerships oversees the representative network across North America, South America, Europe, Africa, and West and Central Asia. It is a global supervisory position, worked through Regional Coordinators and alongside national reps. Tap a region to see what the role does there.

Select a regionFan out the network, then tap a region, or tab to one and press Enter.

Regions are the five the announcement names. Recruiting happens where the network is thin. Fluency in English is required, and additional language ability is a strong advantage given the regions served. This role may be based anywhere in the world.

The internal role

Intake to decision, with nothing falling through

The Research Operations Coordinator owns the intake-to-decision workflow for incoming field submissions and change requests. This is not a purely administrative role. Every accepted change needs a source citation, and the team’s work has to be defensible and transferable. Step a submission through the workflow.
