Clarity for the Nations · Field Report, Abuja

The person documenting the mission may also be the reason the missionary lived through it.

A report from the departure hall in Abuja, after three days with 50 Nigerian leaders in an urban missions research and strategy workshop. What if Luke had never joined Paul? No Acts. No third Gospel. And a man who was stoned at Lystra, shipwrecked, and beaten repeatedly, traveling without the physician who happened to be on his team.

The one rule the workshop runs on

Write what you know, and mark every guess with a G

Participants spent most of Tuesday building an honest profile of one segment they steward. The rule is simple: write what you know, and mark every guess with a G. Every G becomes a research question, because the blanks are findings rather than failures. Work the prompts below on a group you are responsible for. Tap once for known, twice for a guess.

Unlearning before contextualizing

Ten words, banned for thirty minutes

Monday afternoon the room asked the six worldview questions from Scripture itself. Then they had to articulate a biblical worldview clearly and succinctly with ten words removed. The concepts are not banned. The shortcuts are. Tap each word to ban it and watch what is left to work with.

Tap a word to ban it. Tap again to allow it.

The point is to get the cultural wrapping off the gospel and off the church before carrying either one into a new context. On Tuesday, the same six questions get asked of a people group, and the distance between the two sets of answers is where contextualization starts. What the essay did not anticipate was how many missionaries named their own need for unlearning.

Mapping the city

Split it where it fails, then test the pieces again

Abuja is one of the fastest-growing cities in Africa, and much of that growth is in-migration, much of it from the north. So the room mapped it. Name every distinct people in the city, including the ones nobody notices. Test each name against the barriers of understanding and acceptance, split it where it fails, then test the pieces again. Run the test and watch one name become several strategies.

Day three: the plan

Five parts, and who before what

Day three turned it all into a strategy plan, starting with the question every team has to answer before anything else is worth discussing: where does this work actually stand? The Phases of Engagement give the whole body one shared language for that answer. From there, the plan runs on five parts, and the rule underneath all of them is who before what. The line back home

The city is not a detour from frontier work

The frontier has already moved in. But the thing the room refused to give up was the line back home. A believer in the city still has a village network that no urban worker can reach alone, and the traffic runs both directions. Urban ministry and rural ministry among the same people are one work. Conceptual: the argument of the essay, not a measured migration dataset. One conversation the essay did not see coming came out of this exercise. Teams working among prisoners and among women in prostitution started talking with people-group-focused teams, on the straightforward observation that there are people groups inside those populations. Those two rooms had not been introduced before.

What continues after the room

Three days of training is not the deliverable

What happens in the months after it is. The workshop launched monthly coaching and peer learning with everyone who was there. Each call asks what has moved since the last one, and everyone brings the strategy, because those sheets are meant to be revised rather than filed. Switch between the two ways a training can end.

For your team

  1. Would a combined research and strategy workshop serve your network, or are those still two tracks, two audiences, and two budgets?
  2. For the peoples you are responsible for, is your local strategy built on your own research, or on somebody else’s? Do you know what you don’t know?
  3. If you work among a people with both an urban and a rural population, is that one work or two?

The training is built in modules on purpose, so it can be adapted and contextualized to a new setting instead of transplanted. Over the next 6 to 9 months the goal is to run this with national partners in East Africa, Francophone West Africa, and Central Asia. Each carries a different constraint, and the nuance of the workshop will need to change, but the same framework remains.
