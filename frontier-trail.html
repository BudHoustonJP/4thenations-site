FRONTIER TRAIL

The Road to Every People

A journey game for the Great Commission. Choose a path. Make the calls. Face the barriers. The destination is not survival: it is multiplying churches among a people who have never had one.

Choose a setting

30 to 60 minutes · one player or one room

Fictional peoples, real patterns. Every people group in this game is a composite; every barrier is one someone has actually faced.
Built on Joshua Project people group data patterns and the Phases of Engagement Toolkit.

How to Play

You will travel a twelve-stop trail from the first stirring of a call to the day a people group has its own multiplying churches. At each stop you read a scene and choose one of several actions. There are no trick questions. There are trade-offs.

Choose a path

Missionary, Mobilizer, Pastor, or Organization Leader. Each starts in a different place and faces different pressures. All four are aiming at the same destination.

Choose a setting

Guided is coached. Before each decision it tells you what is at issue and how much the decision matters, and after you choose it says at once whether a stronger option was available and why. You can take any decision back and try another. Trail gives no verdict until the end, so the consequences do the teaching. Frontier adds more barriers, withholds the hints, and lets nothing be taken back.

What the game will not do

There is no single right answer to every scene, on any setting. Several options are defensible and the game rewards a pattern of thinking rather than a lookup table. Guided shows you that pattern as you go. Trail and Frontier show it at the end.

Choose a people

Five fictional frontier peoples, each with a profile card and a link to a real people with a similar story. Read the card. It tells you what kind of trail you are on.

Watch five meters

Prayer, Team, Support, Trust, and Health. Your choices move them. If one hits zero, the trail gets very hard.

Expect barriers

Visas get denied. Believers get persecuted. Teams divide. There is no Bible. Barriers come to everyone. What you do next is the game.

Watch the destination

The panel labeled Among the People tracks the people’s Phase of Engagement (0 Waiting to 7 Sustained Gospel Presence), its Engagement Strength, a map of fourteen districts that light up as a witness reaches them, and the raw numbers: believers, churches, generations, local leaders. That is the score that matters.

Light up the accelerators

Twelve tiles track the accelerators you use: prayer, Scripture access, vision casting, collaboration, and eight more. They come from the Phases of Engagement Toolkit. A tile lights the first time you do the thing. Tap any tile, or any accelerator named in an outcome, for its definition and the question it asks.

Three accelerator decisions

Three times, at stops 4, 7, and 10, you choose an accelerator outright: one habit to build for the next stretch. The same accelerator can be exactly right at one phase and a distraction at another, and the game tells you which you picked.

The last stop

Stop 12 is not part of the story. It asks what you will do about a real people, and gives you the link.

Underlined words

Terms with a dotted underline open a definition. Most come straight from Joshua Project; the rest are marked. The full glossary is on the title screen and printed with your report.

Checkpoints and prayer stops

Three times on the trail a checkpoint asks a real question about the unreached world. Right answers help you. Twice you will be asked to stop and pray for a real people through Joshua Project’s Unreached of the Day. That one is worth the most.

The review at the end

On Trail and Frontier nothing is graded during play. At the end, every decision is listed with the option that most served the whole people and one line on why. Guided gives you that same verdict immediately, decision by decision.

Frontier people group: an unreached group with virtually no followers of Jesus and no known movement to Jesus, still needing pioneer cross-cultural workers. Joshua Project approximates this as 0.1% or fewer Christian adherents, fewer than 1 in 1,000. Roughly a quarter of the world lives in such groups.

Keyboard: press 1 to 6 to choose, Enter to continue.

Facilitator Guide

Purpose

Frontier Trail sharpens strategic thinking for the Great Commission: missiology, mobilization, evangelism, church planting, and leadership development, all pointed at one outcome: multiplying churches among a people who have none. The scoring rewards a specific way of thinking. It is never named during play. Its six marks are revealed in the debrief.

Three ways to run it

One room, one screen (45 to 60 min). Project the game. Read scenes aloud. Vote by hand on each choice; the facilitator clicks the majority. Debate is the point. Stop for two minutes whenever a vote splits.

Individual play, shared debrief (60 to 75 min). Each person plays a different path or people on their own device, then compare Trail Reports. Ask: who reached a movement, and what did they do differently?

Replay. Same people, different path. The pastor and the missionary face the same barriers from different chairs.

The toolkit underneath

Phases 0 to 7, Engagement Strength, and the twelve accelerators follow the Phases of Engagement Toolkit. Players learn its vocabulary by using it. If your group works with real people group data, the same phase numbers apply there.

Which setting to run

Guided is a coached tutorial: a stakes line before each decision naming what is at issue, an immediate verdict after it, and take-backs so a group can test two options and compare. Use it for a Sunday class, a youth group, or anyone who wants to learn the pattern rather than discover it. It reveals the reasoning as you play, which means the debrief lands as confirmation rather than revelation: run Trail instead when you want the discovery effect. Guided caps barriers at five. Trail for mission committees, small groups, and candidates. Frontier for staff, coaches, and pre-field workers: up to ten barriers, hints withheld until the report, no take-backs, harsher costs, and a 1.3× score. Run the same people on two settings to show a group how much of the outcome was their own decisions. A good pattern for a long session: play stops 1 to 4 on Guided so everyone learns to read a decision, then restart on Trail with the same people and play it through.

Decisions taken back are counted on the Trail Report, so you can see how much a group explored.

Facilitator view (hidden scoring)

The live scoring panel is behind a passphrase so players cannot stumble into it. To open the prompt, type the word signpost anywhere on the page, or tap the FRONTIER TRAIL wordmark in the top left five times. The default passphrase is romans1520.

Change it by replacing the FAC_HASH value near the top of the script with a new SHA-256 hash. To generate one, open the browser console on this page and run ftHash('your new passphrase').then(console.log). This is a speed bump, not a lock: anyone who reads the file can remove the check. For a group where that matters, hand out a copy with the facilitator block deleted.

The debrief

The ending includes a Trail Report, the six hidden principles with the group's score on each, a next step for each path, and six discussion questions. Print it or screenshot it. The questions are where the training happens.
