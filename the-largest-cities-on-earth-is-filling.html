By 2050, the largest city on earth will be Dhaka. The UN’s World Urbanization Prospects 2025 projects it passing Jakarta at mid-century with about 52 million people. Delhi is projected fourth and Karachi fifth, near 33 million.

Joshua Project places 98.9% of Bangladesh’s population in unreached people groups, 99.1% of Pakistan’s, and 95.9% of India’s. Most of that city growth arrives from the countryside, one family at a time, and each family belongs to a specific people from a specific district.

06 / Research the city for the sake of the village

The road home rarely runs in one hop

Under the UN’s new method, humanity splits three ways. Tap each step on the road home.

UN DESA, World Urbanization Prospects 2025, new method: 45% of humanity in cities, 36% in towns, 19% in rural areas.

Three layers need dedicated attention

Map which peoples live in which parts of the city, how they cluster, and which identity each one leads with away from home.

Name the reachable slices inside each people, such as new arrivals, students, laborers, and the city-born second generation, because each carries its own barriers and openings.

Learn which patterns produce disciples who carry the gospel back to their districts and villages of origin.

The direction

For every priority people group, name the towns and cities it is feeding. Then reverse it: for each growth city, name the people groups arriving and the districts they left. Connect the teams at both ends. That is multi-node engagement at city resolution, and most of it can start from what field contacts already know. Multi-Node Strategy →

Questions for your team

  1. Which towns and cities is your priority people group moving to, and can anyone on your team name the neighborhoods where they land?
  2. If you serve in Dhaka, Karachi, Lahore, or Delhi, which rural districts feed your neighborhood, and does any team working in those districts know you exist?
  3. If you serve in Lagos, Nairobi, Kinshasa, or Dar es Salaam, which unreached people groups now live within walking distance of a national church, and who has asked that church to engage them?
  4. Which of your city efforts has produced a disciple who started something in his or her home district, and has anyone written down how it happened?
