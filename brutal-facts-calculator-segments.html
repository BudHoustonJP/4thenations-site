    How the math works, assumptions, and sources

    The target moves with the population. The tool projects the population forward by births minus deaths (no migration), then solves for the steady annual baptisms needed so the evangelical share lands on the goal as a percent of the larger future population. Growing lostness is built into that growing denominator.

    Every birth is counted as lost. No one is born evangelical. The evangelical population grows only through conversion and loses members through death at the group's death rate.

    The goal options. Two goals show at a time. A group below 2% evangelical can aim to reach 2% or 10% of the future population. Once a group is at 2% or above, the choices become reaching 10% or adding 2 percentage points to the current share. At 10% or above, the choices are adding 2 or 10 points.

    Zero base. A group reporting 0.00% evangelical is treated as 0.001% so the projection has a starting point. Even then the required growth rate runs to the hundreds of percent per year, which is the mathematical case for reproducing movements rather than one-by-one addition.

    Multiplication. Each generation multiplies the disciples by 1 + (share who multiply × new disciples each multiplier makes). The tool finds how long that growth takes to reach the same goal, allowing for population growth along the way.

    Data. Picking a group fills in population, percent evangelical, and percent Christian from Joshua Project, and birth and death rates from the World Bank (2024, by country). You can change any of them. Unreached is 2% evangelical or below and 5% Christian adherent or below; Frontier is 0.1% Christian adherent or below. Figures are estimates for strategy and mobilization.
