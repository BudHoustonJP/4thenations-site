Clarity for the Nations · Strategy & Training

These are not five levels to climb. They are five functions to fill.

Over a decade ago Nathan Shank named five levels of movement leadership. The framework has held up, but one word needs changing. A level is something you climb past and leave below you. A function is a role the body needs filled, all five at once, and every one of them advances the same way: by letting go.

One apple, and then the orchard

The five functions, told through the fruit

Hand someone an apple and you have already handed them the seed. Whether it stays a single piece of fruit or becomes an orchard depends on one thing: whether they know the seed is there, and what it is for. Open each function to see the fruit it works with, and the thing it has to release to reach the next.

Framework adapted from Nathan Shank, 5 Levels of Movement Leadership (Mission Frontiers, 2016). One word changed: functions, not levels.

Every function advances by letting go

Notice what marks the move from one to the next

It is not more control. It is less. Drag from the seed sower to the strategy coordinator and watch the grip loosen while the reach widens. This is why levels misleads: it suggests you gain authority as you rise, when in fact you release it.

The grain of wheat that falls

One apple, released, becomes an orchard

Unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone, but if it dies it bears much fruit (John 12:24). Release the seed and step through the generations. The multiplier's defining mark shows up here: he does not own the fruit.

What actually stalls a movement

The grip, and the open hand

What stalls a movement is rarely a missing function. It is a function gripped too tightly. Toggle between the two, and watch what happens to the field.

Gripped tight
The planter clutches his single tree, kept and subsidized and in charge, and it never becomes an orchard. The problem is not the planter. It is the refusal to release. The cost is a frontier left unseeded.
