Clarity for the Nations · Data & Metrics

Missions data rarely fails because someone lied. It fails because the system rewards a precision we do not have.

Graphs, lists, and maps suggest we can measure exactly where the gospel has gone. The reality is messier. Every number passes through ignorance, money, culture, and human bias before it reaches you. Here is the hidden side of missions data, and the practices that make it honest.

The influence of money

Funding bends the number in both directions

Missions funding follows need, and it also follows fruit. That single fact pulls reporting two opposite ways at once, which is why you cannot correct for it with a simple adjustment. Switch between the two pressures to see who feels each one and what the donor ends up seeing.

Outside accountability is rare. The essay notes reports of entire provinces or nations coming to Christ at scale that are never opened to security-conscious third-party validation, leaving us celebrating claims we cannot check.

The funding paradox

Billions decided on pennies of data

Moving toward more honest reporting

Phases, not percentages

We cannot abandon measurement. Without reporting we would have no sense of where need remains. But a percentage flattens a process into a single digit. The Phases of Engagement are an eight-step continuum, Phase 0 through Phase 7, that tracks where a people group stands in gospel progress, from Waiting to Sustained Gospel Presence. Each phase describes a milestone that is not always reflected well by Christian or evangelical percentage alone. Click any phase to read its definition.

The five practices the essay proposes

  1. Report ranges, not exact numbers. Instead of “0.085 percent Christian,” say “less than 0.1 percent Christian.” Joshua Project has started
  2. Track phases of engagement. Highlight process, not just outcomes. See the Phases of Engagement Toolkit. Data being collected now
  3. Use confidence scores. Add strong, moderate, or low alongside key data points, so users see how much weight a figure should carry. Work in progress
  4. Encourage third-party validation. When extraordinary breakthroughs are claimed, welcome outside verification.
  5. Decouple money from numbers. Support based on vision, faithfulness, and long-term commitment, not unverified reports.
