    How the math works, assumptions, and sources

    What this is. A keep-the-share model. It sizes the baptisms per year needed to hold the evangelical share steady as the population changes, counting new believers to match births, the dilution from non-believer migration, and the net loss of believers through death.

    How it is computed. Per year: share times births, plus migration times the gap between the local share and the believing share of migrants, plus deaths times the gap between the believer share of the dying and the local share. Children of believers are not counted as believers; they need their own conversion.

    The migration lever. Migration is a rate per 1,000 so it scales with population. International-heavy migration lowers the in-migrant evangelical share, which raises the figure.

    The reach goal. Separate from the keep-even number, this solves for the baptisms per day to move the evangelical share to the chosen target over the chosen horizon, accounting for population growth, migration, and believer deaths. Two goals show at a time. A city below 2% evangelical can aim to reach 2% or 10%. From 2% up, the choices are reaching 10% or adding 2 percentage points to the current share. At 10% or above, the choices are adding 2 or 10 points. Most US metros are above 10%.

    Multiplication. Each generation multiplies the disciples by 1 + (share who multiply × new disciples each multiplier makes). The tool finds how long that growth takes to reach the same goal, allowing for population growth along the way.

    The reality check. The baptisms a day figure is your own estimate for the whole city, so it starts empty. Until you add one, the card shows the evangelical share in that tile. When your estimate already covers what holding the share takes, the card says so and points to the goal instead.

    Cities under 2% evangelical. Holding a very small share steady takes very few baptisms, so that number says little. When the evangelical share is under 2%, the big number on the card switches to the goal: what it takes to reach 2% (or 10%) in the years you choose. The hold-steady figure moves to a tile, and the reality check compares your estimate with the goal.

    Definitions matter. Evangelical share here is self-identified, not practicing. State the definition whenever you cite a number from this tool.

    City evangelical estimates. Per-metro shares are starting estimates anchored to Pew's 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study, which reports evangelical share for 34 large metros. Edit any value. For precise figures use the Pew RLS metro pages.

    World cities. Outside the US and Canada the list holds every city of a million people or more (five million or more in South Asia), with 2025 populations from the UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025. The UN now draws each city by its continuous built-up area, so a few names and sizes will look unfamiliar. Evangelical and Christian shares for these cities are country-level figures from Joshua Project people group data, weighted by population. Where Joshua Project publishes no evangelical figure for a country, the Christian share is used as an upper limit. Birth and death rates are World Bank 2024 country figures, and migration starts at zero. A city can differ from its country, so change any value you know better.

    Sources. Metro populations: US Census Bureau 2024 and Statistics Canada 2024 estimates. Evangelical shares: Pew Research Center 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study. Births: March of Dimes PeriStats. Baptism benchmark: Lifeway Research 2024 Annual Church Profile. Figures are estimates for strategy and mobilization, rounded for display.
