Paul never wrote a discipleship curriculum. He left us a word instead: imitate. Yet in the decade-long debate over obedience-based discipleship (OBD) in frontier missions, that word barely shows up.

Both sides argue fiercely about what disciples are taught. Almost nobody asks what they have seen. Scroll through, and work the controls.

Learned, received, heard, seen

Paul’s whole model fit in one sentence

Paul told the Philippians to put into practice whatever they had learned, received, or heard from him, or seen in him (Philippians 4:9). Switch the channels on and off and watch what a disciple actually inherits.

Half the model

Obedience training

Content plus practice.

Nobody learns to chase an intermittent short circuit from a slide deck, or from a list of rules. You get under the hood with someone who has done it a hundred times, and you watch. Then you do it.

Questions for your team

Three questions to take into your next meeting

Open each one. The third asks you to look at your own life. The bar fills as you work through all three.

A process can be modeled in a training room. A life is modeled while fixing dinner, running errands, and handling unscripted interruptions. Imitation requires collapsing the compartments.

The system sets the agenda. The person sets the inheritance. Fidelity to the method tells you about the agenda. What the disciples are becoming tells you about the inheritance.

Tap each part of your life those leaders have actually seen up close, not just heard about.
