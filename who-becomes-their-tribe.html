Clarity for the Nations · Frontier Peoples & the Church

People go where they belong, so when following Jesus costs a new believer their community, something has to take its place.

Decades before the American Revolution, Benjamin Franklin noticed English settlers kept leaving to live with Native American tribes, while Native Americans who lived among the colonists almost never chose to stay. Sebastian Junger’s answer in Tribe: comfort and safety lose to a community that shares danger and needs you. Frontier missions should read that as a warning.

What Junger means by tribe

Not an ethnic group. The people who show up.

Missions already uses “tribe” for ethnic groups. Junger means something smaller. Open each of his three tests and see what changes for a new believer in a Frontier People Group, where religion and family are bound together.

A new disciple should not leave their people group. Pulling believers out of their language and kinship network is a failure in itself, and it removes the gospel from the people who most need to see it lived.

What a new disciple usually loses is narrower and more painful: the religious community that defined belonging within their own people. Something has to form in its place, inside that same people group. That something is the church.

Two ditches

Extraction, isolation, or a church inside the people

New believers fall into two ditches. In a city or diaspora setting, the temptation is extraction. In the village, it is usually plain isolation. Switch between them, then see what the road between the ditches looks like.
